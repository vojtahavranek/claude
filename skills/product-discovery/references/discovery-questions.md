# Discovery Questions Reference

**Auto-loaded during Stage 1 (Product Owner Discovery)**

This reference provides question templates and frameworks for conducting effective product discovery conversations. Use these as guides, not scripts—let the conversation flow naturally.

## Core Discovery Framework: 5Ws + H

### WHO: User Identification

**Primary questions**:
- Who experiences this problem?
- Who are the users we're building this for?
- What are their roles, contexts, or characteristics?
- Are there multiple user segments or personas?

**Deeper exploration**:
- What's their current environment or workflow?
- What's their level of technical sophistication?
- What motivates them? What are their goals?
- How do they currently spend their time?
- What tools do they currently use?

**Persona development**:
- Can you describe a specific person who has this problem?
- Walk me through their typical day
- What frustrates them most about current solutions?
- What would delight them in a solution?

### WHAT: Problem Definition

**Primary questions**:
- What problem are users experiencing?
- What pain points frustrate them?
- What needs aren't being met?
- What's preventing them from achieving their goals?

**Deeper exploration**:
- How severe is this problem?
- What triggers the problem?
- What are the symptoms users experience?
- What happens when the problem occurs?
- Is this a new problem or longstanding?

**Problem clarity**:
- Can you describe a specific instance when this problem occurred?
- What was the user trying to accomplish?
- What went wrong?
- What was the impact?

**Problem scope**:
- Is this problem consistent across all users?
- Are there variations by user segment?
- Does context change the problem?

### WHY: Impact and Importance

**Primary questions**:
- Why does this problem matter to users?
- What's the impact when the problem occurs?
- Why is solving this important?
- What are the consequences of NOT solving it?

**Deeper exploration**:
- How frequently does this problem happen?
- How much time/money/effort does it cost?
- What opportunities are lost?
- How does it affect user satisfaction?
- What's the emotional impact on users?

**Business/organizational impact**:
- Why does this matter to the organization?
- What's at stake?
- How does this align with strategic goals?
- What's the cost of inaction?

**Prioritization**:
- Why now? Why is this important to address now?
- What's changed that makes this a priority?
- What happens if we wait 6 months?

### WHEN: Timing and Triggers

**Primary questions**:
- When does this problem occur?
- What triggers the problem?
- Is there a pattern to when it happens?
- How often does it happen?

**Deeper exploration**:
- What time of day, week, month, year?
- What events or conditions precede the problem?
- Are there seasonal or cyclical patterns?
- Is it predictable or random?

**Workflow timing**:
- At what point in the user's workflow does this occur?
- What are they doing just before?
- What are they trying to do next?
- What's the context?

### WHERE: Context and Environment

**Primary questions**:
- Where are users when this problem occurs?
- What's their physical or digital environment?
- Where in their workflow does this fit?
- Where are they coming from? Going to?

**Deeper exploration**:
- What device/platform are they using?
- What's their location (office, home, mobile)?
- What other tools or systems are involved?
- What constraints does the environment impose?

**System context**:
- Where does this fit in the larger system?
- What upstream/downstream dependencies exist?
- Where are the integration points?

### HOW: Current Solutions

**Primary questions**:
- How do users solve this problem today?
- What workarounds have they developed?
- What alternatives are available?
- How well do current solutions work?

**Deeper exploration**:
- What do users like about current solutions?
- What frustrates them about current solutions?
- Why aren't current solutions sufficient?
- What would they change about current solutions?

**Workaround analysis**:
- What manual steps do they take?
- What tools do they cobble together?
- How much effort does the workaround require?
- What's the pain level with workarounds?

**Competitive analysis**:
- What competitive solutions exist?
- Why do/don't users choose them?
- What can we learn from competitors?
- What gaps do competitors leave?

## Product-Type-Specific Templates

### Consumer Products (B2C)

**User behavior questions**:
- How do users discover solutions like this?
- What motivates them to try new solutions?
- What causes them to abandon solutions?
- How do they make purchasing decisions?

**Adoption patterns**:
- What's the barrier to entry for new users?
- How quickly can users get value?
- What causes users to become power users?
- What drives word-of-mouth?

**Value perception**:
- What would users pay for this?
- How do they perceive value?
- What's the alternative cost (time, money, effort)?
- What's their willingness to pay?

### B2B / Enterprise

**Organization needs**:
- Which teams or departments need this?
- How does this fit organizational structure?
- What's the approval/procurement process?
- Who are the decision makers vs users?

**Buyer personas**:
- Who controls the budget?
- What are their purchasing criteria?
- What's the typical sales cycle?
- What proof points do they need?

**Enterprise concerns**:
- What security/compliance requirements exist?
- What integration requirements exist?
- What support/training is expected?
- What's the rollout process?

**ROI justification**:
- How will they measure success?
- What metrics matter to stakeholders?
- What's the expected ROI timeframe?
- What risks do they perceive?

### Developer Tools

**Developer experience focus**:
- What's the learning curve?
- How does this fit their workflow?
- What's the time to first value?
- How does it integrate with existing tools?

**Technical context**:
- What languages/frameworks/platforms?
- What scale/performance requirements?
- What are common use cases?
- What edge cases exist?

**Adoption drivers**:
- Why would developers choose this over alternatives?
- What makes developers stick with a tool?
- What causes tool abandonment?
- How do developers discover tools?

### Internal Tools

**Team workflows**:
- What's the current workflow?
- Who's involved in the process?
- Where are the bottlenecks?
- What manual steps exist?

**Efficiency metrics**:
- How much time does current process take?
- What's the error rate?
- How many people are involved?
- What's the productivity impact?

**Adoption challenges**:
- Will teams actually use this?
- What resistance might exist?
- What training is needed?
- How do we ensure adoption?

**Organizational context**:
- How does this align with team priorities?
- Who are the stakeholders?
- What's the approval process?
- What budget is available?

### Integrations / APIs

**Technical constraints**:
- What systems need to integrate?
- What APIs or protocols are involved?
- What data needs to flow?
- What are the technical limitations?

**Compatibility**:
- What versions need to be supported?
- What platforms/environments?
- What backwards compatibility is required?
- What breaking changes are acceptable?

**Use cases**:
- How will developers use this integration?
- What are the common patterns?
- What are the edge cases?
- What error scenarios exist?

## Discovery Conversation Patterns

### Socratic Questioning Techniques

**Clarifying assumptions**:
- "What are we assuming about users?"
- "What if that assumption is wrong?"
- "How do we know that's true?"

**Exploring implications**:
- "If we solve this, what happens next?"
- "What would success look like?"
- "What could go wrong?"

**Probing deeper**:
- "Can you tell me more about that?"
- "Why is that important?"
- "What's underneath that?"
- "Give me an example"

**Challenging gently**:
- "What if we approached this differently?"
- "Have you considered...?"
- "What about users who...?"
- "Could there be another explanation?"

**Revealing hidden needs**:
- "What would the perfect solution look like?"
- "If you had a magic wand, what would you create?"
- "What would users be willing to give up for this?"
- "What's the one thing that must work?"

### Follow-up Prompts for Deeper Understanding

**After initial problem description**:
- "Walk me through the last time this happened"
- "Show me (or describe) what you see/experience"
- "What were you trying to accomplish?"
- "What did you expect to happen?"
- "What actually happened?"

**After solution description**:
- "How would a user accomplish [task] with this?"
- "What happens if [edge case]?"
- "Walk me through the user's first experience"
- "What's the 'aha!' moment?"

**After business rationale**:
- "What data supports this?"
- "Who else has succeeded with this approach?"
- "What would change your mind about this?"
- "What's the biggest risk?"

### Pivot Indicators and Questions

**Red flags that suggest pivot needed**:
- User problem is unclear or not validated
- Solution doesn't actually address stated problem
- Users say they want it but behavior suggests otherwise
- Business case doesn't hold up under scrutiny
- Technical feasibility is questionable
- Better alternative emerged during discovery
- Competitive landscape changed understanding

**Questions to explore pivot**:
- "What if the real problem is actually...?"
- "What if we focused on [alternative] instead?"
- "Could we address this from a different angle?"
- "What if we narrowed/broadened the scope?"
- "Is there a simpler way to deliver value?"

**Pivot framing**:
- "Based on what we've discussed, I'm wondering if..."
- "Another approach might be..."
- "What if we pivoted to focus on...?"
- "The conversation revealed that maybe..."

### Red Flags (STOP Recommendation Indicators)

**Problem not validated**:
- Can't clearly articulate user problem
- Problem is hypothetical, not observed
- Users don't actually experience pain
- Problem is low severity/frequency

**Solution misalignment**:
- Solution doesn't address root cause
- Solution addresses symptom, not problem
- Users wouldn't use proposed solution
- Solution is a technology looking for a problem

**Business/organizational concerns**:
- No clear market or user base
- Competitors have failed with similar approach
- Internal stakeholders not supportive
- Resources unavailable or insufficient
- ROI doesn't justify investment

**Technical concerns**:
- Feasibility highly questionable
- Dependencies unavailable or unreliable
- Scale/performance requirements unrealistic
- Integration complexity too high

**User commitment**:
- User is uncertain or uncommitted
- User keeps changing direction
- User can't articulate why it matters
- User won't commit to using it

**Questions to probe STOP decision**:
- "What would happen if we didn't build this?"
- "What's the cost of waiting 6 months?"
- "What evidence supports moving forward?"
- "What would need to be true for this to succeed?"
- "What's your confidence level this will work?"

## Tips for Effective Discovery Conversations

### Do

✅ **Listen actively**: Hear what user is really saying, not just words
✅ **Ask open-ended questions**: "Tell me about..." not "Do you...?"
✅ **Follow curiosity**: Explore interesting threads
✅ **Validate understanding**: Repeat back what you heard
✅ **Probe beneath surface**: Ask "why" multiple times
✅ **Use specific examples**: Request concrete instances
✅ **Embrace silence**: Give user space to think
✅ **Challenge assumptions gently**: Question respectfully
✅ **Iterate and refine**: Revisit earlier points with new insights

### Don't

❌ **Lead the witness**: Don't suggest answers in questions
❌ **Jump to solutions**: Understand problem first
❌ **Ignore red flags**: Address concerns directly
❌ **Rush through themes**: Take time for depth
❌ **Assume you know**: Ask even if seems obvious
❌ **Get defensive**: Stay curious, not protective
❌ **Avoid difficult questions**: Ask the hard questions
❌ **Accept vague answers**: Push for specificity

### Balancing Act

**Depth vs breadth**: Cover all themes, but go deep on critical areas
**Structure vs flow**: Use framework as guide, not rigid script
**Challenge vs support**: Question assumptions while supporting exploration
**Speed vs thoroughness**: Match complexity level, don't rush critical thinking

## Complexity-Specific Guidance

### Quick Mode (Simple Features)

**Focus on**:
- Clear problem articulation
- Basic user understanding
- Simple solution description
- Quick validation check

**Questions to prioritize**:
- WHO needs this? (basic)
- WHAT problem? (clear articulation)
- WHY important? (brief)
- HOW do they solve it now? (quick)

**Validation**: Light touch, focus on feasibility

**Time**: 10-15 minutes total

### Standard Mode (Medium Features)

**Focus on**:
- Thorough problem exploration
- Multi-faceted user understanding
- Detailed solution visualization
- Solid business validation

**Questions to prioritize**:
- All 5Ws + H covered
- Multiple user scenarios
- Edge cases considered
- Business case validated

**Validation**: Balanced, appropriate to project type

**Time**: 15-20 minutes for discovery

### Deep Mode (Complex Features)

**Focus on**:
- Comprehensive problem analysis
- Multiple user segments
- Complex solution considerations
- Rigorous validation

**Questions to prioritize**:
- All questions applicable
- Deep probing on every theme
- Multiple scenarios and edge cases
- Thorough competitive analysis
- Risk assessment
- Dependency mapping

**Validation**: Comprehensive and rigorous

**Time**: 25-35 minutes for discovery
